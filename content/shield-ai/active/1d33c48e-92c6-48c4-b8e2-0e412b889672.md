### Job Description:

The Assistant Product Owner (APO) supports Shield AI's delivery of mission autonomy (MA) to the U.S. Air Force (USAF) Collaborative Combat Aircraft (CCA) program — the first time that USAF has decoupled, for procurement purposes, MA from the airframe as a standalone capability. In this context, the ‘product’ is the Autonomy Implementation package, including Mission Autonomy itself, the Human Machine Interface (HMI) software, A-GRA compliance, mission systems, training, cybersecurity, and the full suite of implementation/installation software.

Shield AI’s CCA programs adopt an agile framework and are organized as a leadership tier overseeing multiple shoals (from platform integration to autonomy capabilities), each with multiple feature crews. In the first instance, the APO will support the Platform Integration and Support shoal (Shoal 1), which is responsible for integrating MA with multiple platforms and with the operator interface. The APO will work in collaboration with the Shoal Lead, Systems Engineering Lead, and four Feature Crew Leads and report directly to the Product Owner/Manager, who is part of the program leadership team. The APO owns the day-to-day health of the Shoal 1 product backlog in Jira (and Confluence), and is expected to maintain an integrated picture of how customer priorities, epics, interfaces, dependencies, and milestones relate, and to make ‘product’ legible and accessible to the broader team.

### What you'll do:

* Work with the Product Owner and Chief Engineer to understand customer product needs (across the CCA program office, platform providers, and operational end users) and to determine priority, separating must-have capability from deferrable scope
* Elicit and validate needs and priorities from the customer, including official guidance and scoping documents, and from operational subject matter experts or surrogate end users as available
* Translate customer intent into customer needs into user stories and epic descriptions — including value proposition, success criteria, acceptance criteria, expected effort and timeline, dependencies, risks, and preconditions for success — against which feature crews can execute their sprints
* Own day-to-day management of the Shoal 1 epic backlog across feature crews, including maintaining traceability with customer requirements, refining the backlog, and monitoring backlog updates to ensure changes in priority or scope are surfaced and their rationale documented appropriately
* Provide a product perspective to feature crews at Shoal 1-specific sprint planning and review ceremonies, by maintaining an informal integrated product map of epics, interfaces, dependencies, and program milestones
* In collaboration with the Shoal Lead, manage dependencies across shoals, and carry integration decisions and associated implementation implications through to the respective shoal or feature crew leads, or collaboration partner
* In collaboration with the Shoal Lead, support development and integration of the operator interface, primarily through the backlog — the HMI feature crew owns delivery, the Shoal Lead owns requirements and acceptance, and the APO owns description, communication, and prioritization of epics
* Keep protected weekly hands-on time in the operator interface, so that HMI-specific epics and instructions can be written from first-hand use rather than second-hand reports
* Support the platform integration crew in their interaction with vendors
* Act as translator across disciplines — normalize preferred terminology and make specialist discussion accessible to the whole team, ensuring general consistency
* Support other shoals (e.g., Systems Engineering, T&E) through observation of operator-in-the-loop (OITL) activity and by providing feedback in review sessions
