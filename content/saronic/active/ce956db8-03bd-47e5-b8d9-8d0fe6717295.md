Saronic Technologies is a leader in revolutionizing autonomy at sea, dedicated to developing state-of-the-art solutions that enhance maritime operations through autonomous and intelligent platforms.

# **Job Overview**

Saronic is building the autonomous surface fleet of the future — purpose-built platforms that extend the reach, lethality, and resilience of the U.S. Navy, U.S. Coast Guard, and allied maritime forces. As we scale, the regulatory, licensing, and operational approval environment we operate in is becoming as consequential to our mission as the technology itself.  
  
We are hiring a Senior Manager of Maritime Regulatory Affairs & Mariner Credentialing to serve as Saronic's primary liaison to the U.S. Coast Guard, the Department of Homeland Security, and the Department of War, and to own our mariner licensing, credentialing, and Navigation Rules training program. This is primarily a regulatory and operations liaison role, with a meaningful credentialing and training component. You will partner closely with our Maritime Operations leadership, our Head of Regulatory & Litigation, our General Counsel, and our government affairs and policy teams to shape the regulatory and operational environment in which Saronic's uncrewed vessels are built, tested, and deployed.  
  
This is not a legal or traditional compliance role. It requires deep fluency in Coast Guard authorities and culture, sound operational judgment, the ability to translate novel autonomous-vessel operations into frameworks regulators can approve, and the discipline to manage many parallel approvals, inspections, and credentialing actions without slowing the fleet down.

# **Responsibilities**

**USCG/DHS Regulatory & Operations Liaison**

* Serve as Saronic's primary interface with USCG Headquarters (CG-5P, CG-ENG, CG-CVC, CG-MMCP), the Marine Safety Center, the National Maritime Center, Districts, Sectors, and OCMIs, and with DHS components including TSA and CBP.
* Coordinate test, demonstration, and transit approvals with Sectors and Captains of the Port, including safety zones and regulated navigation areas (33 CFR Part 165), marine event permits (33 CFR Part 100), VTS coordination, and Notices to Mariners.
* Coordinate with Department of War and Navy stakeholders on test ranges, fleet exercises, and operating areas where Coast Guard and DoW authorities overlap.
* Lead design-basis agreements, equivalency and alternative-compliance requests, and plan-review engagements for Saronic's uncrewed and optionally crewed platforms.
* Manage marine casualty, hazardous-condition, and mishap reporting with Legal (46 CFR Part 4, 33 CFR 160.216), and own follow-through with investigating units.

**Regulatory Compliance & Operational Standards**

* Standardize CONOPS and regulatory reviews across Maritime Operations, including implementation of USCG work instructions for uncrewed and autonomous vessels (e.g., 5P-WI-002(1)).
* Maintain compliance with the 46 CFR and 33 CFR requirements that apply to Saronic's fleet and sites, including vessel inspection and certification, the Navigation Rules (COLREGs / 33 CFR Part 83), ISM and safety management systems, and MTSA vessel and facility security (33 CFR Parts 101–106).
* Build audit-ready documentation, inspection readiness, and corrective-action processes across test sites and operating locations.

**Licensing & Mariner Credentialing**

* Own Saronic's credentialing and training framework for USV operators, remote supervisors, and vessel crews, defining when Merchant Mariner Credentials, STCW endorsements, and internal certifications are required and how training is documented for Coast Guard acceptance.
* Develop and deliver Navigation Rules training (COLREGs and Inland Rules) for USV operators and remote supervisors, including how the Rules apply to uncrewed and remotely supervised operations, and maintain training and assessment records.
* Manage MMC and TWIC applications, renewals, medical certificates, and sea-service documentation through the NMC and TSA, and grow the share of credentialed operators as the fleet scales.
* Administer the DOT/USCG drug and alcohol testing program (46 CFR Part 16, 49 CFR Part 40) and maintain its records.
* Work with training providers and the NMC on course approvals and competency standards for remote and autonomous operations.

**Policy, Rulemaking & International Engagement**

* Support Legal and Government Affairs on rulemaking comments, Coast Guard policy development, and legislative proposals affecting uncrewed maritime systems, including autonomy-equivalency and manning provisions.
* Represent Saronic before federal advisory committees and industry bodies (e.g., NAVSAC, MERPAC, ASTM and RTCM working groups).
* Coordinate with international flag administrations and regulators, including the UK Maritime and Coastguard Agency, and track IMO developments such as the MASS Code.
* Advise senior leadership on regulatory risk, opportunity, and strategy.

**Cross-Functional Leadership**

* Advise engineering, product, operations, and business leaders on how design and operating decisions affect regulatory approvals and crewing requirements.
* Build and lead the regulatory and credentialing team and infrastructure of a fast-scaling defense technology company.

# **Qualifications**

* Former U.S. Coast Guard officer with DHS and Department of War experience strongly recommended.
* 15+ years as a USCG Prevention officer (e.g., Marine Inspector, Investigating Officer, Sector Prevention, OCMI staff, NMC, MSC, or CG-5P) or similar maritime regulatory experience, including leadership roles.
* Hands-on experience with merchant mariner credentialing, including MMCs, STCW endorsements, TWIC, medical certificates, and sea-service evaluation (46 CFR Parts 10–12).
* Experience developing or delivering training on COLREGs and the Inland Navigation Rules.
* Working knowledge of 46 CFR and 33 CFR, the drug and alcohol program (46 CFR Part 16), ISM and Subchapter M, and MTSA, and familiarity with USCG policy and work instructions for uncrewed and autonomous vessels.
* A track record of securing novel approvals, equivalencies, or waivers from the Coast Guard or other maritime regulators.
* Strong writing and speaking skills: able to draft clear regulatory submissions, policy papers, and briefings, and to brief Coast Guard leadership, DHS and DoW officials, field inspectors, mariners, and senior executives with equal credibility.
* This is a non-lawyer role; a JD or bar admission is not required.
* Sound judgment, low ego, high ownership, and the temperament to thrive in a fast-moving, mission-driven company.

**Desirable**

* USCG Marine Inspector, Investigating Officer, or Port State Control qualifications.
* Existing relationships across USCG Headquarters, Districts, Sectors, the NMC, DHS, and the Department of War.
* Experience with international flag administrations, classification societies, or IMO delegations.
