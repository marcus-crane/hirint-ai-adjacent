Saronic Technologies is a leader in revolutionizing autonomy at sea, dedicated to developing state-of-the-art solutions that enhance maritime operations through autonomous and intelligent platforms.

## Role Summary

**Team:** Production Engineering · **Reports to:** Production Engineering Manager in the Port Alpha Operations team

The Production Engineer makes Saronic's ships easier and cheaper to build by putting Production's best practices into Engineering, Design, and Planning at the earliest stage. You will own the preferred build methods for your discipline (for example piping, electrical, HVAC, structures, joinery, or welding). You will back each method with time-study and cost-benefit data, and you will check that Functional and Detail Design products follow those methods before they are released to Production.

You will work in a small, cross-functional team of subject-matter experts. You will help define the Facilities Manual and the Production Manual, which are the standards the shipyard builds to. Your work will shape the greenfield facility design and every follow-on hull.

## Key Responsibilities

**Design-build standardization**

• Define and publish Production's preferred build methods for your discipline

• Back every preferred method with a time study or cost-benefit analysis that shows the least rework and cost while still meeting quality standards.

• Review in-progress designs on an ongoing basis to confirm the applicable preferences to Production disciplines are captured and match the current Production Manual.

**Gate review**

• Gate-review finalized Functional Design and Detail Design products before release to Production.

• Check each product against the Production Manual and Facilities Manual. Hold or reject products that miss the preferred methods, and return them with the specific corrective action required.

• Record gate-review outcomes so repeated rejections point back to gaps in the manuals or the standardization process.

**Manuals and facilities**

• Contribute to the Production Manual / Handbook as the single source of truth for best practices.

• Contribute to the Facilities Manual by defining the capabilities, equipment, and workshop requirements your discipline needs at each construction stage. Prioritize them against the greenfield design-lock timeline.

**Cost-down and continuous improvement**

• Champion cost-down initiatives across all phases of design and build. Prove ROI before any change enters the manual, including products introduced to meet ABS or other classification requirements.

• Capture lessons learned from the lead ship and drive them into follow-on hulls to improve safety, quality, time, and cost.

**Digital production information**

• Help define the preferred digital production data methods for your discipline. These include model-based definition vs. 2D drawing authority, 3D work instruction formats, and what "released for production" data looks like.

**Field liaison**

• Support the liaison engineering process for field-discovered issues, and help set which fixes count as simple design corrections.

• Feed recurring field fixes back into the Production Manual so a repeated workaround becomes a standard design solution.

## Required Qualifications

• Bachelor's degree in Naval Architecture, Marine, Mechanical, Electrical, Structural, Industrial, or Manufacturing Engineering, or equivalent experience.

• Hands-on shipbuilding or heavy-fabrication experience in at least one trade discipline: piping, electrical, HVAC, structures, joinery, or welding. You should know how design choices affect build cost, rework, and schedule.

• Working knowledge of the production process from fabrication and sub-assembly through unit assembly, erection, and outfitting.

• Ability to run or apply time studies and cost-benefit analysis, and to justify a standard with data rather than preference.

• Experience reading and reviewing 3D models and drawing packages against a build standard.

• Familiarity with marine and military design standards and classification requirements

• Clear written and verbal communication. You can give design engineers specific, defensible corrective actions and hold a position when a release is not ready.

• Comfortable in a fast-moving, early-stage environment where standards are being written for the first time.

## Preferred Qualifications

• Experience on a lead ship or first-of-class build, including capturing lessons learned for follow-on hulls.

• Experience with autonomous or robotic welding, or designing for automated fabrication.

• Experience with model-based definition, 3D work instructions, or digital work packages on the shop floor.

• Experience in liaison engineering or field engineering, resolving build issues through design corrections.

• Experience with greenfield facility planning, including bay sizing, crane capacity, and workshop layout.

• Experience with pre-outfitted modules, such as toilet and shower units and staterooms, and with modular build strategies.

## Success Measures and Working Relationships

Success in this role means Production's preferred methods are in the design before the drawings reach the shop floor.

• Preferred methods for your discipline are published in the Production Manual, each backed by time-study or cost-benefit data.

• Finalized Functional and Detail Design products pass gate review with fewer rejections over time.

• Rework and field-driven corrections go down on follow-on hulls, and lessons learned are captured in the manuals.

• Your facility requirements are captured before the greenfield design locks.

You will work most closely with the Production Engineering Manager and the other Production Engineering specialists (Safety, Autonomy, Piping, HVAC, Electrical, Structures, Welding, Industrial Engineering, Facilities/Autonomy Build, Digital Production Information, and Liaison). You will also work with Functional and Detail Design Engineering, who receive your gate-review findings, and with Production leadership and the shop floor, who build to your standards.
