# About the Team

The Firmware team owns the embedded software layer that powers every hardware subsystem of our humanoid robot from distributed communication bus architecture and power management to actuator and motor drive control. We sit at the intersection of hardware and software, and our work is directly on the critical path to every product milestone: without reliable firmware, the robot cannot move, charge, or operate safely. If you want to build the foundational software for a new category of physical AI, this is where that work happens.

# Your Charter

Own the motor control firmware that turns current into motion in every joint of NEO, and make it robust, safe and maintainable. Your control loops decide how smooth, quiet and compliant NEO feels around people, and how fast the team can move as the actuator platform grows.

# Key Outcomes

* Develop motor control firmware for BLDC/PMSM actuators, including field-oriented control (FOC), space-vector PWM, and current, velocity and position loops
* Integrate and calibrate position sensing: absolute and incremental encoders, Hall sensors, commutation offsets and multi-turn tracking
* Tune control loops for high bandwidth, low torque ripple and low acoustic noise, including feedforward, friction and cogging compensation
* Implement torque and impedance control modes that support safe, compliant interaction with people
* Build fault detection and protection for overcurrent, overvoltage, undervoltage, overtemperature, stall and sensor failures, with safe torque-off behavior
* Design motor drive communication with deterministic timing to the central controller
* Bring up new motor driver boards and support hardware design reviews for gate drivers, current sensing and power stages
* Test and validate firmware releases on the bench and on full robots, and build tools for automated tuning, logging and regression testing

# Key Competencies

* Strong embedded instincts: you write control code that stays correct under real hardware conditions, including ISR timing, ADC sampling alignment, PWM edge cases and DMA conflicts
* First-principles debugging: you find root causes at the hardware-firmware boundary with a scope, current probe and an understanding of motor physics
* Safety-first mindset: you treat fault handling, current limits and safe torque-off as core firmware, not an afterthought
* Clear cross-functional communication: you explain control and firmware constraints to electrical and actuator engineers, and pull in early during hardware design

# Minimum Requirements:

* 5+ years of embedded firmware development in C/C++ (7+ for senior roles)
* Shipped motor control firmware for BLDC or PMSM motors, including FOC and cascaded control loops
* Solid grasp of motor physics and power electronics: inverters, gate drivers, current sensing, dead time and PWM
* Experience with encoders and sensor interfaces such as SPI, BiSS-C, SSI or ABZ
* Hands-on experience with EtherCAT/CAN or another real-time fieldbus
* Comfort with interrupt-driven, hard real-time code on MCUs such as STM32, TI C2000 or similar
* Hardware debugging with oscilloscopes, current probes, logic analyzers and dynamometers

# Preferred Skills

* Experience with robotic joints, quasi-direct-drive or high-ratio geared actuators
* Background in sensorless control, field weakening or motor parameter identification
* Experience with EtherCAT or CAN FD at high update rates (1 kHz and above)
* Experience with safety-critical systems or standards such as IEC 61508, ISO 13849 or ISO 26262, including functional safety concepts like STO
* Familiarity with a real-time OS such as FreeRTOS, SafeRTOS or Zephyr
* Modeling and simulation in MATLAB/Simulink or Python for controller design
* Robotics or autonomous systems background
