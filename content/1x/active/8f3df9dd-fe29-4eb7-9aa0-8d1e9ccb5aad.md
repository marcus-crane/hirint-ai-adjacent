# **About the Team**

Our Hardware Engineering team is responsible for developing the mechanical systems that enable NEO to move safely, efficiently, and reliably in the real world. We work across mechanical design, structural mechanics, materials science, actuation, sensing, controls, manufacturing, and reliability to develop highly integrated robotic hardware.

Tendon-driven systems are a critical part of how we transmit force and motion throughout NEO. We develop these systems from fundamental material characterization and early prototypes through validation and production-ready hardware, solving challenging problems in rope mechanics, routing, wear, fatigue, friction, packaging, and reliability.

# **Your Charter**

Build the tendon systems that make humanoid robots move.

You will own the design, prototyping, characterization, and optimization of tendon-driven transmission systems for NEO. You will develop routing architectures, structural components, rope interfaces, terminations, pulleys, capstans, and test systems while solving the complex mechanical challenges associated with transmitting loads through lightweight, compact robotic systems.

This is a highly hands-on role. You will design in CAD, perform structural analysis, build prototypes and custom test rigs, execute experiments, investigate failures, and translate what you learn into better hardware.

Your work will directly impact NEO’s strength, range of motion, efficiency, weight, reliability, lifetime, and overall physical performance.

# **Key Outcomes**

* Design and release robust tendon-driven transmission systems and routing architectures for NEO
* Develop lightweight structural designs that meet demanding strength, stiffness, packaging, fatigue, and reliability requirements
* Characterize rope materials, constructions, terminations, wear mechanisms, tension profiles, friction, creep, and thermal behavior through structured experimentation
* Design and build custom mechanical and electromechanical test rigs to quantify tendon lifetime, fatigue, efficiency, and failure modes
* Optimize rope routing, pulleys, capstans, interfaces, and termination strategies to improve system efficiency and durability
* Select and validate high-performance synthetic fibers, braided ropes, coatings, termination hardware, and related materials for robotic applications
* Identify root causes of tendon and mechanical system failures and implement design changes that improve reliability and lifetime
* Develop production-ready CAD, drawings, GD&T, tolerance analyses, test methods, and technical specifications
* Partner with controls, electrical, manufacturing, reliability, and materials teams to integrate tendon systems with actuators, sensors, and the broader robot architecture
* Establish a rigorous experimental and technical knowledge base that accelerates future tendon-system development
