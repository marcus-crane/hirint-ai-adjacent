# **About the Team**

The Hands team builds the hands that let NEO grasp, manipulate, and help around the home. Packed with motors, encoders, gearing, and sensors, NEO’s hands are among the most dexterous and highest-cycle subsystems on the robot, and every one of them has to be proven before it ships.

Test engineering sits at the center of that work, from early bench characterization and design validation through production test and field returns. We partner closely with electrical, firmware, mechanical, and manufacturing engineering to make sure what we design is what we build, and that it keeps working.

# **Your Charter**

Build and own the tests that prove NEO’s hands work. You will develop the test software, stations, and procedures that characterize hand actuators and sensors, catch failures early, and keep production moving with confidence.

As a Hardware Test Engineer, you’ll bridge the bench and the production line, understanding what each test is actually measuring, turning raw data into clear root causes, and building tests that technicians can run reliably at scale.

# **Key Outcomes**

* Build automated pass/fail test scripts that log data and generate plots to characterize hand actuators, encoders, and sensors with repeatable, trustworthy results
* Own and maintain test PCs and production stations, keeping them healthy, networked, and running validated software
* Turn failures into answers by pulling logs, pinpointing the moment a failure happened, and writing clear root-cause summaries that drive design and process fixes
* Write repeatable procedures and build tests that technicians can run safely and consistently on live production equipment
* Design and build test fixtures and jigs that speed up validation of hands, grippers, and high-cycle actuators

# **Key Competencies**

* Strong electromechanical intuition with a clear understanding of what a test is actually measuring, not just how to run it
* Rigorous, data-driven debugging with the ability to trace a fault from a log or waveform back to its source in hardware, firmware, or the test itself
* Production mindset: careful with live equipment, disciplined about procedures, and focused on repeatability
* Strong cross-functional collaboration skills and comfort working across hardware, firmware, and software boundaries
* Ownership mindset with a bias toward execution, accountability, and continuous improvement
