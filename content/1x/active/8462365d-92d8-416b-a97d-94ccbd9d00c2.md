**About the Team**

**Your Charter**

Own the camera stack NEO sees through, from the sensor and lens selected for the robot to the calibrated, rectified frames every perception model consumes. This is critical-path work: no vision model is better than the images it is handed, and nothing downstream survives a camera that is miscalibrated, badly tuned, or quietly dropping frames. You will own that path from vendor selection through end-of-line calibration on the production line and out to robots already working in homes.

**Key Outcomes**

* Own camera selection end to end: define sensor, lens, and module requirements with the hardware and perception teams, run the trade studies, and carry a choice through vendor engagement and bring-up on real hardware
* Deliver a camera pipeline that holds up in production: sensor drivers, multi-camera synchronization and timestamping, and frame delivery that meets its latency budget without dropping frames or destabilizing timestamps
* Tune the image path for machines rather than for people: exposure, white balance, and dynamic range chosen so perception models are accurate in real homes, including the low light and hard backlight a living room actually presents
* Make calibration a production process, not a lab procedure: intrinsics and extrinsics calibrated on the line inside cycle time, validated automatically, monitored for drift across the fleet, and recoverable in the field

**Key Competencies**

* **Camera systems depth** understanding sensors, optics, and ISP as one system, and how an image travels a robotics stack from capture through transport, compression, and transformation to the model that consumes it
* **Lives at the hardware boundary** debugging across MIPI CSI-2, serdes links, I2C, PoE, and power and clock sequencing, and treating dropped frames, timestamp instability, and bandwidth limits as everyday failure modes rather than mysteries
* **Calibration rigor** treating calibration as measurement with error bars, reasoning about tolerances and uncertainty, and knowing how a calibration error propagates into what a perception model gets wrong
* **Thinks in fleets, not units** designing calibration and diagnostics that survive a production line at volume and robots already in homes, not just a bench with a careful operator

**Minimum Requirements**

* 5+ years shipping camera or imaging software in production systems: robotics, autonomous vehicles, consumer devices, or AR and VR
* Hands-on experience bringing up image sensors on embedded Linux, including V4L2, MIPI CSI-2, and the driver and device tree work that comes with it
* Demonstrated experience with camera intrinsic and extrinsic calibration, including validating calibration quality rather than trusting a solver's output
* Strong C++ in performance-critical systems, with the profiling and debugging habits to find where frames and milliseconds actually go

**Preferred Skills**

* Experience with ISP tuning and image quality evaluation, especially where the consumer is a perception model rather than a person
* Experience taking calibration into manufacturing: fixture and station design, cycle time, yield, and failure analysis on build data
* Familiarity with CUDA or GPU image processing and zero-copy image transport between processes
* Background in multi-camera synchronization, stereo rectification, or camera-IMU time alignment
