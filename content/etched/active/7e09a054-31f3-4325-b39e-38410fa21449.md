**About Etched**

Etched is building hardware for frontier intelligence. We co-design chips, racks, software, and manufacturing to deliver best-in-class throughput and latency across both prefill and decode workloads. Our first products are heavily focused on **inference**. Backed by hundreds of millions from top-tier investors and staffed by leading engineers, Etched is redefining the infrastructure layer for the fastest growing industry in history.

**Job Summary**

Etched is hiring a Manufacturing Design Engineer to drive factory execution and manufacturability for our hardware systems. In this role you will own the transition from product design into repeatable, high-yield manufacturing at our contract manufacturers and manufacturing partners.

You will work closely with hardware, electrical, mechanical, test, quality, operations, and supplier teams to ensure our products are ready for build, debug, ramp, and sustained production. This is a highly hands-on role for someone who thrives in fast-moving factory environments and can bridge design intent with manufacturing reality.

**Key Responsibilities**

* Lead manufacturing readiness for new product introductions from prototype builds through ramp and sustaining production.
* Serve as the on-the-ground manufacturing engineering owner for FATP-style factory execution in Taiwan.
* Drive DFM and DFT across board, subsystem, and system-level hardware to enable robust, scalable, and cost-effective production.
* Partner with CM to define and improve assembly, integration, and test processes.
* Develop and release assembly instructions, SOPs, tooling, fixtures, and process documentation.
* Support board and system bring-up, hands-on debug, and failure analysis during engineering builds and production.
* Identify manufacturing bottlenecks and drive rapid issue resolution to protect build schedules, yield, and quality.
* Analyze production data to improve yield, throughput, cycle time, cost, and quality.
* Lead root cause investigations and corrective actions for process, assembly, and test failures.
* Review and influence Process FMEA and Design FMEA, and help identify manufacturing risks before ramp.
* Work with test, quality, and supplier teams to implement inspection strategies, error-proofing, and process controls.
* Evaluate factory readiness, including line setup, station deployment, operator training, and equipment utilization.
* Drive continuous improvement initiatives across factory operations, including scrap reduction, line balancing, and automation opportunities.
* Communicate clearly and concisely with Etched engineering teams and external manufacturing partners.

**You may be a good fit if you have (Must-have qualifications)**

* Bachelor’s or Master’s degree in Mechanical Engineering, Electrical Engineering, Manufacturing Engineering, Industrial Engineering, or a related field.
* 5+ years of experience in manufacturing engineering, process engineering, NPI, or factory operations for complex hardware systems.
* Experience supporting server, compute, electronics, or other high-complexity system builds in a contract manufacturing environment.
* Strong understanding of system assembly flows, PCBA manufacturing, final assembly, integration, and production test.
* Proven ability to work across prototype, NPI, ramp, and sustaining phases.
* Strong hands-on debugging and problem-solving skills, especially in factory or build environments.
* Experience improving yield, quality, and throughput through data-driven process changes.
* Ability to work effectively with cross-functional engineering teams and external manufacturing partners.
* Ability to work full-time onsite in our CM factory and travel to different manufacturing sites as needed.

**Strong candidates may also have experience with (Nice-to-have qualifications)**

* Experience with fixture design, tooling validation, line bring-up, or automation deployment.

**How we’re different**

Etched believes in the [Bitter Lesson](http://www.incompleteideas.net/IncIdeas/BitterLesson.html). We think most of the progress in AI has come from using more FLOPs to train and run models, and the best way to get more FLOPs is to build model-specific hardware. Larger and larger training runs encourage the world to consolidate around fewer model architectures, which creates a market for single-model ASICs.

We are a fully in-person team in San Jose and Taipei, and we greatly value engineering skill. We do not have hard boundaries between engineering disciplines, and we expect our technical staff to contribute wherever needed.
