## About the Team

The Battlespace Awareness (BA) Radar team develops radar systems that address national security challenges for America and its allies. We own the full product lifecycle, from concepts and architectures through fielding and sustainment. Working across hardware, software, and firmware, in both the RF and digital domains, we turn raw sensor data into detections, measurements, tracks, and useful mission decisions. You will work with an interdisciplinary team to develop new capabilities, test prototypes, and mature those prototypes into products.

## About the Job

We are looking for a Radar Research Scientist to develop mathematically principled algorithms, physics-based models, and analysis tools that improve radar performance. You will turn a mission or engineering question into a tractable technical problem, build a working prototype or simulation, and use data to determine whether the result holds up.

The work connects propagation and scattering, antenna and RF behavior, waveforms, signal processing, detection, and tracking. You might investigate a discrepancy between simulation and field data, model the effect of multipath or hardware impairments, or evaluate a new detection or estimation method. Your results will help hardware and software engineers understand what limits performance and decide what to build next.

Our modeling and analysis code is primarily Python. Strong mathematical foundations, thoughtful experimentation, and clear explanations matter more than prior radar experience. Candidates from communications, physics, applied mathematics, or related sensing fields can bring valuable perspectives. This is a hands-on scientific-computing role, not a requirement to own production C++, CUDA, or RTL.

## What You'll Do

* Formulate mathematical and physical models that answer practical questions about radar performance, with explicit assumptions and limitations.
* Prototype signal-processing, detection, estimation, array-processing, or tracking algorithms and compare them against existing methods.
* Develop and extend end-to-end radar simulations, at the fidelity the question requires, with reproducible scenario configurations connecting target and environmental models, antenna and RF behavior, and digital processing.
* Choose appropriate model fidelity and use analytical bounds, parameter sweeps, and Monte Carlo experiments to quantify performance and uncertainty.
* Analyze I/Q, range-Doppler, detection, and track data to investigate discrepancies between predictions and measurements, identify limitations, and recommend focused experiments.
* Build reusable analysis and visualization tools that make large sensor datasets easier to explore and understand.
* Provide validated reference implementations, representative datasets, assumptions, and numerical tolerances so algorithms, software, firmware, and hardware engineers can turn prototypes into product improvements.
* Maintain tested, reproducible scientific code and communicate assumptions, results, and limitations clearly to colleagues and stakeholders.
