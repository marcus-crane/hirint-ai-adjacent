**ABOUT THE TEAM**  
  
The Air Dominance and Strike Low Observables Team are advancing into the next generation of LO Technology to enhance the survivability and effectiveness of unmanned air systems. Our Low Observables engineering team is responsible for designing, analyzing, testing, and sustaining stealth features that reduce radar (RCS) and infrared (IR) signatures, ensuring our systems remain undetectable in contested environments.  
  
**ABOUT THE ROLE**  
  
This is an early career role open to engineers with a degree in aerospace, electrical, or mechanical engineering. Low Observables work sits at the intersection of all three: the shape of the air vehicle, the electromagnetic behavior of its surfaces, and the materials and processes used to build it.  
  
You do not need prior stealth experience. Almost nobody arrives with it, because most of it cannot be taught outside a cleared program. We will teach you the LO-specific methods. What we are looking for is strong engineering fundamentals, comfort picking up unfamiliar analysis tools, and the judgment to tell a real result from a bad one.  
  
You will be paired with an LO engineer and start on a scoped piece of a live program, not a training exercise.  
  
**WHAT YOU'LL DO:**

* Run broadband Radar Cross Section (RCS) analysis on components and full air vehicles, and learn to judge when a prediction can be trusted
* Support the design and development of Low Observable unmanned air systems, from early configuration trades through detailed design
* Build and improve the analysis tools, scripts, and validation workflows the team runs on
* Model LO materials and treatments, and help select and optimize them for specific components
* Support manufacturing and validation planning, including tolerances, inspection methods, and what is actually producible at rate
* Help plan and execute RCS measurement campaigns in indoor chambers and on outdoor ranges, then reduce and interpret the data
* Compare measured results against predictions, chase down the disagreements, and feed what you learn back into the models

**REQUIRED QUALIFICATIONS**

* Must be pursuing or have recently completed a bachelor’s or master’s degree in Electrical Engineering, Aerospace Engineering, Mechanical Engineering, or a related field.
* Must be a U.S. Person due to required access to U.S. export-controlled information or facilities; U.S. clearance eligibility may be required depending on program.
* Able to start in 2027.

**PREFERRED QUALIFICATIONS:**

* Coursework in at least one of: electromagnetics or RF, aircraft or vehicle design, or materials and manufacturing processes
* Working knowledge of a scripting or analysis language such as MATLAB, Python, or C++
* Familiarity with a 3D CAD package such as NX, CATIA, SolidWorks, or Creo
* Demonstrated hands-on engineering work: a capstone or senior design project, internship, undergraduate research, or a student competition team
* Ability to explain a technical result clearly, in writing and in a design review
* Exposure to any electromagnetic simulation tool, such as HFSS, CST, FEKO, or COMSOL. X-Patch, SENTRi, and McMoM2D are government codes and we do not expect you to have used them
* Coursework or project experience with composites, additive manufacturing, casting, or injection molding
* Experience with laboratory RF measurement: network analyzers, anechoic chambers, or antenna pattern measurement
* Experience reducing, visualizing, and sanity-checking measured data sets
* A prior internship or co-op in aerospace, defense, or another hardware-heavy environment
