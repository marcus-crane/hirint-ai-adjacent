Anduril Industries is a defense technology company with a mission to transform U.S. and allied military capabilities with advanced technology. By bringing the expertise, technology, and business model of the 21st century’s most innovative companies to the defense industry, Anduril is changing how military systems are designed, built and sold. Anduril’s family of systems is powered by Lattice OS, an AI-powered operating system that turns thousands of data streams into a realtime, 3D command and control center. As the world enters an era of strategic competition, Anduril is committed to bringing cutting-edge autonomy, AI, computer vision, sensor fusion, and networking technology to the military in months, not years.

## About the Team

The Mission Command team is part of the Connected Warfare Division. We build the command and control (C2) tools that help warfighters understand what is happening around them, make faster decisions, and direct their forces. Our work spans the full Lattice portfolio from tactical edge applications to the integrations and data infrastructure that connect them. We spend a lot of our time with the people who actually use these tools: in the field, during exercises, wherever they are.

## About the Role

This is not a business development role that happens to live near the customer. The engagement work is the product introduction work. As a Strategic Engagement Lead for USARPAC, you own the path from first contact with a unit or command to a shared understanding of what Lattice and the Connected Warfare portfolio can do for their mission and what it cannot.

Your primary audience is Corps and Division Commanders and their staffs across U.S. Army Pacific. You walk into a room with a two-star or a one-star, often for the first time, and explain what Anduril builds, how it fits into their operational picture, and what preparation looks like before fielding. You translate complex technical capabilities into language that resonates with senior leaders who think in terms of mission, risk, and readiness.

Hawaii is the center of gravity for USARPAC and INDOPACOM’s Army component. The units here train for large-scale combat operations across the Pacific theater, operate in austere and contested environments, and work alongside joint and allied partners daily. You need to understand that context deeply, build trusted relationships within it, and use those relationships to shape how Anduril’s capabilities are understood and adopted.

The profile we are hiring for: someone who can walk into a formation, understand the fight, earn the trust of the people leading it, and credibly connect Anduril’s technology to the problems that keep them up at night. You are not selling—you are advising. You back that up with real curiosity, domain knowledge, and a bias for action.

## Key Responsibilities

* Drive NGC2 engagement from the top-down.
* Prepare the battlespace for Army formations receiving NGC2. Validate or redefine Anduril delivery assumptions and ensure customer org structures support NGC2 as a priority.
* Cultivate senior leader advocacy and institutional adoption of NGC2 across Army formations.
* Create and operationalize a repeatable engagement model across Army formations. Evangelize lessons learned from previous customers with new customers early (C2SE for change management, exercise schedule, etc).
* Develop mid-term (6-month) NGC2 product roadmap based on operational leadership priorities. Identify priority applications, features, and integrations and populate product roadmap ahead of fielding.
* Identify efficiencies and opportunities in NGC2 product integration across software, transport, and compute layers based on identified Warfighting needs.
* Evangelize 1P Anduril products within Army formations. Cultivate customer champions for Anduril products.
* Step in as senior Anduril leader for escalation or deescalation when executive leadership engagement is required.

## What You’ll Do

* **Own the introduction to senior leaders.** Conduct initial engagements with Corps and Division Commanders and their staffs across USARPAC, explaining what Lattice and the Connected Warfare portfolio are, what they do, what they don’t do, and what it takes to get ready for fielding.
* **Build trusted relationships across USARPAC.** Earn the trust of commanders, chiefs of staff, G3/G6 staffs, and subject matter experts. Become a partner who understands how the unit actually fights and operates in the Pacific theater—not just what shows up in a requirements document.
* **Know the operational environment.** Develop deep familiarity with USARPAC’s mission set, the INDOPACOM theater, and how Army forces organize, train, and operate across the Pacific. Understand the unique challenges of distributed operations, contested logistics, and multi-domain integration in this AOR.
* **Translate technology into operational language.** Turn complex product capabilities—across applications, data models, integrations, and infrastructure—into clear narratives that connect to how a commander thinks about mission command, fires, maneuver, and sustainment.
* **Shape the path to fielding.** Advise units on what preparation looks like before they receive Anduril capabilities: network readiness, training requirements, integration with existing systems, and realistic expectations for what the technology will and won’t change on day one.
* **Coordinate across Anduril.** Pull together product, engineering, field service, and business development teams around a coherent engagement plan. Ensure what we say in the room matches what we can deliver, and feed what you learn back into the product organization.
* **Brief well and tell the story.** Communicate clearly to a few staff officers, a full command brief, or a GO-level one-on-one. Adjust the level of detail for the audience without losing the substance. Your materials should be authoritative, concise, and operationally relevant.
* **Run the room.** Lead workshops, working groups, and planning sessions. Get useful information out of a room full of senior leaders, settle competing views, and leave with decisions and next steps.
* **Stay connected through fielding.** Maintain relationships with units as they move from introduction through fielding and operational use. Track whether the story you told in the first meeting holds up under real conditions, and feed lessons learned back to the team.
* **Spot patterns and scale what works.** Identify engagement approaches, materials, and narratives that work and package them so the broader team can use them. Don’t reinvent the wheel for every unit.

## Who You Are

Anduril moves fast and is scaling fast. We value ambition, hard work, a high bar, and a willingness to disrupt how things are normally done. The people who do well here tend to show these traits:

* **Bias for Action.** You ask for forgiveness, not permission, and you hate waiting around to be told what to do.
* **Outcome-Oriented.** You figure out what the mission needs and work backward from there.
* **Customer Focused.** You have real empathy for the warfighter and you build trust by solving their problems, not pitching products.
* **Dive Deep.** You sweat the details and get into the weeds to understand the operational problem before proposing a solution.
* You care about the outcomes your engagements produce and hold yourself accountable for follow-through.
* You change direction without drama when the priorities change.
* **First Principles Thinking.** You solve problems based on what is actually best here, not because a vendor recommended it or because it is how your last company did it.
