## **About Anthropic**

Anthropic’s mission is to create reliable, interpretable, and steerable AI systems. We want AI to be safe and beneficial for our users and for society as a whole. Our team is a quickly growing group of committed researchers, engineers, policy experts, and business leaders working together to build beneficial AI systems.

# **About the role**

Anthropic is hiring an EHS Manager, Data Center Construction and Operations to own environment, health, and safety across the full lifecycle of our data center sites — from construction and commissioning through to live operations. As we scale the physical infrastructure behind our research, this role makes sure the high-hazard work of building and running data centers is done safely, by our partners and for our people.

This is a specialized, field-facing, program-ownership role, and contractor safety is at the center of it. Most of the hands-on work at these sites is performed by general contractors, subcontractors, and specialty trades, so your impact comes through how well you set expectations, qualify and oversee those partners, and hold multi-employer worksites to our safety standards. You'll be Anthropic's safety presence on the ground — through active construction, the transition of commissioning, and the shift into operational EHS once a site is live.

You'll own these programs end to end today, and as our data center footprint grows you may build and lead a team to support them. You won't be doing this alone: you'll partner closely with the EHS Lead — who will help you stand up the vendor, consultant, and specialist support you'll rely on — and work with our construction, infrastructure, operations, Security, and Legal partners, as well as the contractor and operator organizations running the sites.

# **Responsibilities**

Contractor safety is the core of this role. You'll build and run the programs that govern how contractors work on our sites — prequalification and selection, safety plan and method-statement review, site-specific orientation, high-hazard work permitting, and the audits and inspections that verify the work matches the plan. On multi-employer worksites you'll set clear expectations, coordinate across general contractors and subcontractors, and hold every party accountable for working safely.

You'll own EHS across the construction, commissioning, and operations phases. During construction that means the high-hazard exposures data centers involve — electrical and energized systems, working at height, heavy equipment, cranes and rigging, excavation, and fire and life safety — governed by OSHA construction (29 CFR 1926) requirements. Through commissioning you'll manage the safety of energization and testing, and as sites go live you'll transition programs to operational safety under general industry standards (29 CFR 1910), including electrical safety and NFPA 70E, lockout/tagout, and arc flash.

You'll investigate incidents, near misses, and injuries across your sites, drive them to root cause, and follow through on corrective actions — and you'll own EHS record keeping and reporting across the portfolio, including OSHA requirements and any contractor-injury tracking. You'll be the owner's safety representative in the room with construction and operations partners: reviewing readiness, walking sites, escalating when work isn't safe, and stopping it when it needs to stop.

You'll drive a consistent safety culture across geographically distributed sites and different partner organizations — building emergency preparedness, running or overseeing drills, and making sure lessons from one site reach the others. You'll select and manage the external support these programs depend on — third-party safety, industrial hygiene, and audit partners — with the EHS Lead's help in setting those relationships up, and you'll contribute to our site design and safety standards so new builds start from a safer baseline.

## **How we're different**

We believe that the highest-impact AI research will be big science. At Anthropic we work as a single cohesive team on just a few large-scale research efforts. And we value impact — advancing our long-term goals of steerable, trustworthy AI — rather than work on smaller and more specific puzzles. We view AI research as an empirical science, which has as much in common with physics and biology as with traditional efforts in computer science. We're an extremely collaborative group, and we host frequent research discussions to ensure that we are pursuing the highest-impact work at any given time. As such, we greatly value communication skills.

The easiest way to understand our research directions is to read our recent research. This research continues many of the directions our team worked on prior to Anthropic, including: GPT-3, Circuit-Based Interpretability, Multimodal Neurons, Scaling Laws, AI & Compute, Concrete Problems in AI Safety, and Learning from Human Preferences.
