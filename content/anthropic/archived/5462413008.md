## **About Anthropic**

Anthropic’s mission is to create reliable, interpretable, and steerable AI systems. We want AI to be safe and beneficial for our users and for society as a whole. Our team is a quickly growing group of committed researchers, engineers, policy experts, and business leaders working together to build beneficial AI systems.

## **About the Role**

As the Technical Program Manager for Government Partnerships, you'll own Anthropic's day-to-day operational engagement with NIST’s US Center for Advancing Innovation and Standards for Super Intelligence (NIST CAISSI), the UK AI Security Institute (AISI), and other key government partners. These institutions evaluate our models, collaborate with our researchers, and are essential partners in advancing the safety and security of AI. You'll run what makes that work possible.

This role will require extensive collaboration both with our government partners and with many teams across Anthropic notably our teams involved in policy, safeguards, model launches, and research on areas such as alignment, monitorability, cybersecurity, and biosecurity.

Things that make someone a great fit for this job: deep understanding of government and ideally specific understanding of these agencies, skill with delivering fast-paced tactical work, and strategic understanding of how Anthropic and our government partners can mutually achieve our objectives.

## **Responsibilities:**

* **Own the day-to-day relationship** - Serve as the primary operational contact for all interactions between Anthropic and these government partners. Run regular meetings with our partners, triage every request, build the trackers and playbooks that let the work scale both for consistency with these partners and where valuable to more government partners.
* **Manage model access end to end** - Ensure each partner has the access they need, under the right agreement, at the right time for the duration of time they need the access.
* **Coordinate legal agreements** - Work with Legal to negotiate, amend, and maintain MOUs, evaluation plans, research plans, NDAs, and data-sharing agreements that govern the work.
* **Drive partner testing throughout the model launch process** - Partner with launch teams to brief the institutes on upcoming models, deliver technical reports, and answer questions fast. Get findings to the researchers who need them in time to use them. Bring a point of view on how to align partner testing to impact within Anthropic and provide input where requested by partners on the research and evaluation directions which are uniquely valuable to Anthropic.
* **Coordinate research collaborations** - Identify opportunities to connect government researchers with Anthropic researchers where there is mutual interest. Scope the work, keep it moving, and align government partner research efforts to where their work has the most impact.
* **Manage publications and incidents** - Coordinate review of system card text, blog language, and partner reports with Communications and Legal. Lead coordination when testing surfaces a vulnerability or incident or when Anthropic conducts an incident disclosure.

## **How we're different**

We believe that the highest-impact AI research will be big science. At Anthropic we work as a single cohesive team on just a few large-scale research efforts. And we value impact — advancing our long-term goals of steerable, trustworthy AI — rather than work on smaller and more specific puzzles. We view AI research as an empirical science, which has as much in common with physics and biology as with traditional efforts in computer science. We're an extremely collaborative group, and we host frequent research discussions to ensure that we are pursuing the highest-impact work at any given time. As such, we greatly value communication skills.

The easiest way to understand our research directions is to read our recent research. This research continues many of the directions our team worked on prior to Anthropic, including: GPT-3, Circuit-Based Interpretability, Multimodal Neurons, Scaling Laws, AI & Compute, Concrete Problems in AI Safety, and Learning from Human Preferences.
