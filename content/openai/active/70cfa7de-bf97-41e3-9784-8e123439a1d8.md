**About the Team**

OpenAI’s Robotics team builds hardware and software for general-purpose robots. We work closely with AI researchers and use what we learn from testing on physical robots to guide the next design.

**About the Role**

We’re hiring an Engineering Manager to lead engineers working on firmware, low-level controls, and systems software for onboard compute. You’ll develop the people doing this work and shape the roadmap with technical leads and partners in hardware and research. You’ll stay close enough to the engineering to help the team make difficult calls, whether that means running one more experiment on the bench or deciding that a change is ready to ship.

**In this role, you will:**

* Hire and coach engineers, give them clear feedback, and help them grow. Give experienced engineers room to lead designs and create opportunities for others to take on technical ownership.
* Set priorities with technical leads and partners in hardware and research. Decide how to staff the work, communicate tradeoffs early, and resolve dependencies that could hold up a hardware milestone.
* Guide work on embedded systems, from board bring-up and device interfaces to actuator control. Invest in reusable systems software so other engineers can integrate hardware, command it, and understand how it responds.
* Set the bar for changes that ship to physical robots. Use design reviews, hardware-in-the-loop tests, and measurements of timing, tracking accuracy, and fault recovery to judge readiness.
* Bring the right people together when a problem crosses disciplines. If an actuator misses its target under load, help the team choose the measurement that will distinguish a controls problem from a firmware or mechanical one.

**You might thrive in this role if you:**

* Have managed a team that shipped and supported software on physical hardware. You can point to engineers you hired or coached who went on to lead significant work of their own.
* Bring depth in firmware, embedded operating systems, or real-time controls, and can reason across all three. Experience integrating custom hardware or off-the-shelf robotic platforms is valuable.
* Have taken a robotic or electromechanical system from prototype through repeated use. You know when a quick bench test will answer a design question and when reliability needs to be demonstrated under load.
* Have led work across teams with different priorities. You can make a clear decision as requirements change, explain it to partners, and give engineers the context and autonomy to carry it through.
