**About the Team**

OpenAI’s Technology Strategic Sourcing team helps Engineering, Research, and other technology teams secure the supplier capabilities they need to scale. We work with technical, finance, legal, security, and business leaders to shape commercial strategy, negotiate critical agreements, and build resilient supplier relationships.

**About the Role**

Own sourcing strategy for infrastructure software and the Engineering and Research stack. You’ll manage billion-dollar-scale spend, personally lead the most complex commercial negotiations, and deliver measurable savings while protecting the capabilities Engineering and Research rely on.

This is a principal-level individual contributor role with no direct reports at launch. You’ll lead cross-functional deal teams and coach sourcing peers. People management experience is a plus; the role may expand to include management as the team and spend grow.

Build multi-year category strategies across data platforms, observability, developer tools, and software for machine learning research, training, and inference. Use technical roadmaps, demand, and market intelligence to decide where to build, buy, consolidate, compete, or transition. Develop credible alternatives before they’re needed.

Build lasting relationships with senior Engineering and Research leaders and supplier executives, including suppliers that are also OpenAI customers. Work with Sales, Finance, and Legal to align buying and selling strategies while evaluating each decision on its own merits.

This role is based in San Francisco, CA. We use a hybrid work model of 3 days in the office per week and offer relocation assistance to new employees.

**In this role, you will:**

* Personally lead large, complex infrastructure software and technology negotiations, from developing leverage and modeling deal economics through executive alignment and signature.
* Keep deals, renewals, dependencies, and decisions organized. Set clear owners and milestones, communicate proactively, and surface risks early enough to act.
* Find commercial opportunities before requests or renewals come in. Use demand, usage, pricing, and market data to improve commitments, licensing, unit economics, or supplier mix.
* Partner with Engineering and Research leaders, existing Sourcing owners, and Finance, Legal, Security, IT, and Corporate Development as relevant to align the deal strategy and approval path.
* Lead 360-degree supplier reviews covering spend, commitments, performance, technical dependencies, risk, and the broader partnership.
* Shape complex deal structures with cost, equity, revenue, partnership, risk, and long-term strategic implications.
* Advise executive leadership on where to invest, renegotiate, or walk away. Present credible options, financial and operational trade-offs, and a clear recommendation.
* Manage strategic suppliers beyond signature, including performance, renewals, escalations, and transitions. Plan migration milestones, overlap costs, exit rights, and fallback options with technical owners.
* Deliver significant savings and better unit economics, and validate results with Finance against comparable baselines.
* Build negotiation and supplier-governance practices peers can use independently.

**You might thrive in this role if you:**

* Have 10+ years of experience leading complex commercial negotiations in strategic sourcing, technology procurement, strategic partnerships, deal strategy, corporate development, or a related field.
* Use AI in recurring sourcing workflows, such as market analysis, scenario modeling, or negotiation prep. Verify outputs, protect sensitive information, and own the final judgment.
* Have led experienced peers and cross-functional deal teams without relying on formal authority. You can show how you clarified ownership, coached others, and improved commercial outcomes across multiple complex workstreams.
* Have personally led large, complex infrastructure software or technology contracts with sophisticated pricing and licensing, capacity or usage commitments, service levels, and commercial protections.
* Understand enterprise software business models, including usage-based pricing, multi-year commitments, ramp structures, pooled consumption, growth protections, and performance-based economics.
* Have led complex relationships with suppliers that are also customers. You can evaluate buying economics and customer commitments separately, identify where the two create value or risk, and advise executive leadership on how to proceed.
* Understand how ML experimentation, training, and inference affect demand, cost, reliability, and supplier dependencies; use that knowledge in commercial decisions.
* Bring executive presence on both sides of a deal. You can explain the economics and technical trade-offs plainly, recommend a path, and hold a position with senior leaders and suppliers under pressure.
* Take initiative, keep complex workstreams organized, and communicate recommendations, decisions, and risks without being asked.
