We’re training and deploying frontier models for enterprises who are building AI systems. We believe that our work is instrumental to the widespread adoption of AI and we are looking for folks that want to be part of that.

We are a global technology company headquartered in Toronto with key offices in London, New York City, San Francisco, Montreal, Paris, Berlin and Seoul. Join us!

## Why this role?

We're hiring a Technical Program Manager to own model delivery.

Every model we ship, whether we trained it or brought it in from a partner, has to be selected on evaluation criteria, integrated and routed across the product stack, and adhere to regulatory requirements; released to customer environments ready for general availability. That now happens on a monthly cadence, with several model evaluations a week, and each release goes out to our platform partners.

This is a standing program, with one owner from model candidate to GA. You will join Product Engineering Program Management and work across modeling, evaluation, inference serving, engineering, customer delivery.

At Cohere, Technical Program Managers are the operational owners of cross-functional work. The best of them are precise at the tactical level and useful at the strategic one: organized, execution-oriented, pragmatic, and able to change pace when the program does.

## As a Technical Program Manager, you will:

* Own the path from model candidate to general availability for first-party and third-party models, on a monthly cadence.
* Build one release process across modeling, evaluation, product integration, and customer delivery, with an owner, exit criteria, and lead time for each stage.
* Run a delivery-readiness gate that has to pass before general availability: packaging, signed images and digests, documentation, serving configuration, test coverage, and parity across Platform, Model Vault, and private deployments.
* Work with Safety and Legal to build regulatory checkpoints into the model release pipeline, including EU AI Act systemic-risk obligations. Program manage the recurring compliance calendar post launch: the yearly review of our frontier framework, serious-incident reporting deadlines, and access for external evaluators.
* Stand up a model registry the field, partner teams and regulators can answer from, covering name, route, alias, checkpoint, sizing, serving configuration, and where each model is available.
* Run intake for new models, keep the pipeline visible, and drive the selection decision to the right owner by a date, with a decision log of which models enter the lineup.
* Sequence deprecation and partner notices on a calendar agreed with customer and partner teams.
* Make the monthly model lineup change a routine step in North, including routing and availability, and feed model readiness into the product release train.
* Build the mechanisms that make the cadence repeatable: templates, the registry, automation, and retros that change the system.
* Write the status, the decision, and the external notice so executives, engineers, and partners are working from the same answer.

## You may be a good fit if you:

* Have at least 8 years as an Engineering or Technical Program Manager, including ownership of repeatable launches with readiness checklists, go/no-go gates, and rollback criteria that teams actually used.
* Can discuss model serving and deployment at a working level: GPU sizing, tensor parallelism, quantization, speculative decoding, containers, Kubernetes and Helm, image signing, and air-gapped or private deployment. Enough to spot conflicting answers and know who decides.
* Drive open questions (name, alias, default, sizing) to the owner who should decide them, by a date, and then publish the answer.
* Influence without authority across research, engineering, product, and the field.
* Write clearly for executives, engineers, and external partners, including customer and partner notices.
* Are comfortable at a high cadence, with shifting priorities and overlapping launches.
* Can read evaluation results and a cost-versus-performance tradeoff, and can tell when a benchmark does not answer the product question.

## **Helpful, and not required:**
