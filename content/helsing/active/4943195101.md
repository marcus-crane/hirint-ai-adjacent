## **The role**

You will be part of a team building the cutting edge and production-grade software foundations that power autonomous robotics. Your work will focus on designing, implementing, and deploying high-performance ROS2-based architectures and control software for robots operating in complex, unstructured, and dynamic real-world environments. This includes ensuring low-latency real-time communication, robust sensor integration, and reliable mechatronics performance. You will own the software lifecycle from architecture design to physical deployment on custom hardware, ensuring that our systems are highly reliable, easily diagnosable, and ready for critical real-world operations.

## The day-to-day

* Design, implement, and rigorously test the ROS2 architecture and C++ modules that allow autonomous platforms to perceive, decide, and act reliably in complex, unstructured, and often adversarial environments.
* Take architectural concepts from a whiteboard to a fielded system, working hands-on with custom mechatronics to integrate sensors and actuators so the software layer performs exactly as required by the hardware layer.
* Develop diagnostic tooling and failure-analysis pipelines to detect actuator and system anomalies before they become mission failures, treating reliability as a foundational requirement.
* Utilize simulation environments such as MuJoCo and Isaac Sim, alongside Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL) testing, to stress-test control logic and de-risk deployment, ensuring what works in simulation survives contact with the real world.
* Enforce high standards through unit and integration testing alongside robust CI/CD pipelines, treating the codebase as critical infrastructure that prioritizes clarity, modularity, and long-term maintainability.
* Package and ship production-grade software via containerized environments like Docker directly to the edge compute hardware running on the physical robots.
* Spend time directly in the test environment, working alongside the physical hardware to diagnose edge cases that only surface when software meets the real world.
