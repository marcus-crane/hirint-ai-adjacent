## **The role**

As a Systems Engineer for C-UAS System-of-Systems, you own the high-level architecture of a distributed air defence capability.

You define the top-level architecture (L0 and L1), partitioning the capability into network, sensor, and effector subsystems and the interfaces between them. The architecture is open and hardware agnostic: any qualified node can join the network. It is controlled centrally but is designed to keep operating when nodes are lost or contested, so that no single point of failure breaks the chain. This is the layer that enables engage-on-remote and launch-on-remote, where the effector need not sit with the sensor that cued it.

This is a role for someone who thinks at the level of the whole system. Your decisions on architecture, networking, and interfaces determine whether a distributed capability performs as one coherent whole under real operational conditions.

## The day-to-day

* Own the top-level (L0/L1) architecture of the C-UAS system-of-systems, partitioning the mission into network, sensor, and effector subsystems
* Specify the network capabilities that let distributed nodes share a common air picture and coordinate engagements, including decentralised operation when the central command node is degraded or absent
* Derive network, sensor, and effector requirements from the operational concept, and own the trade-offs between coverage, resilience, latency, and cost
* Translate operational concepts into a coherent system architecture and clear engagement concepts, then hold that architecture consistent as the design matures
* Keep the architecture open and hardware agnostic, establishing the interfaces that let any qualified node join the network
* Enable engage-on-remote and launch-on-remote by defining how cueing, track handover, and fire control flow across nodes
* Produce a structured architecture that can be exposed externally to partners and customers at the right level of abstraction, without disclosing sensitive detail
* Collaborate daily with the situational awareness and interceptor integration teams, and with AI, software, and hardware engineers, to keep the system-of-systems coherent
