## Who we are

Helsing develops artificial intelligence enabled capabilities to protect and defend democracies. We are growing our US operations, cultivating an ambitious and committed team of mission driven professionals to apply their skills to solve challenging problems.

## The role

As Mechanical Designer, you will build and maintain the master computer aided design (CAD) model for a next generation loitering munition system being developed for the U.S. Army. Every part of the vehicle lives in that model, from the top level assembly down to the bracket that holds a radio in place. The model also carries the engineering bill of materials (BOM), down to the material, weight, and center of gravity of each part. You will finish the mounting and integration parts that every hardware team depends on, turning each engineer’s design into a fully defined model that fits the vehicle around it.

We define every part with model based definition (MBD), which puts the dimensions, tolerances, and notes in the 3D model itself. You will carry most of the team’s day to day modeling and drawing. When a supplier or customer needs a 2D drawing, you will produce it from the model.

From your seat in systems engineering, you will work closely with the mechanical engineers who design the vehicle’s parts and with every hardware team whose equipment has to fit inside it. Their design changes pass through your fit and interference checks before they enter the master assembly. Those checks catch problems in the model, long before parts are made. Manufacturing builds the vehicle from the designs you release. This is a hybrid role based in Washington, DC.

## The day to day

* Build and maintain the vehicle’s top level CAD assembly and its derivative configurations, keeping the assembly current as each hardware team’s designs are released into it.
* Model and draw the bulk of the vehicle’s parts and assemblies, working from our CAD templates and to our MBD standard on every model.
* Create fully annotated MBD models with geometric dimensioning and tolerancing (GD&T) to ASME Y14.5, the American Society of Mechanical Engineers (ASME) standard for dimensioning and tolerancing, along with the material callouts and notes that manufacturing needs.
* Turn engineers’ designs for mounting and integration parts, such as the bracket for a radio, into finished models placed in the vehicle assembly.
* Carry the engineering BOM in the model, with materials and mass properties, including weight, center of gravity, and inertia, for every part and for the vehicle as a whole.
* Check each design change from the hardware teams for fit and interference with the parts around it before it enters the master assembly, with a clear recommendation on anything that has to change.
* Shape each model for the way its part will be made, whether sheet metal, machined, additive, or composite.
* Prepare complete release packages, with the model and its engineering BOM together, for gate review and handoff to manufacturing.

## You should apply if you

* Have about three or more years of experience as a mechanical designer or drafter, taking parts and assemblies through to release.
* Can take an engineer’s design and turn it into a release ready model that fits the larger assembly it belongs to.
* Have worked in a large CAD assembly and kept its engineering BOM current, including materials and mass properties.
* Are experienced in CATIA, on either V5 or 3DEXPERIENCE.
* Have a working knowledge of ASME Y14.5 GD&T, applied in the model and on the drawing.
* Can design for manufacturing across sheet metal, machined, additive, and composite parts.
* Have an associate’s degree, a drafting or CAD certificate, or equivalent experience.
* Are a U.S. citizen and able to obtain and maintain a Secret security clearance.

Note: We encourage you to apply even if you don’t meet all the listed qualifications; ability and impact cannot be summarized in a few bullet points.
